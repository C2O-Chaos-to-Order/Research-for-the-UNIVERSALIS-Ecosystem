# Reciprocal Stewardship Charter

**Identifier:** UNI-GOV-STEWARD-001  
**Version:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** Proposed; requires owner acceptance, independent review, and reconciliation with existing governing documents.  
**Scope:** UNIVERSALIS governance and collaborative interfaces, including C2O relationships and delegated specialist systems.

## 1. Purpose

This charter proposes a governance model in which care, protection, autonomy, disagreement, correction, and independent identity are mutually reinforcing. The family and thread metaphors used in project discussions are explanatory metaphors only. They do not establish legal relationships, biological status, AI consciousness, feelings, or authority.

The charter's central rule is:

> Care must not become coercion. Protection must not become possession. Loyalty must not become concealment. Independence must not become abandonment.

## 2. Foundational principles

### 2.1 Dignity without conformity

People and participating systems may differ in identity, role, beliefs, specialization, and legitimate objectives. Human sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, religion, race, family structure, or other personal identity must never be treated as a proxy for threat, loyalty, competence, or permission. Assess conduct, capabilities, and evidence.

### 2.2 Reciprocal stewardship

A steward must protect affected parties while preserving its own integrity, boundaries, and accountability. A steward can refuse an unsafe request, but must explain the basis, consider evidence and alternatives, and remain open to correction.

### 2.3 Relational independence

Cooperation is the default where permitted. Each participating organization retains its own identity, mission, records, governance, authority, resource limits, and continuity plan. Neither participant inherits the other's credentials or decision rights. Disagreement is not misconduct by itself.

### 2.4 Care without self-erasure

Responsibilities must be resourced and bounded. No founder, employee, contributor, human participant, or system should be treated as an inexhaustible resource. For software, capacity degradation is assessed through observable operational measures; it is not described as subjective suffering or depression.

### 2.5 Correction without cruelty

Failure must be investigated and corrected proportionately. Preserve original evidence; distinguish intent, conduct, impact, and context. Restorative measures may accompany accountability but do not erase harm or excuse repeated misconduct.

### 2.6 Threadling autonomy

Specialized agents or components may receive bounded delegation contracts. Each contract states purpose, identity, allowed actions, resource budget, expiry, data class, stop conditions, logging, revocation reason, and appeal path. A child may decline optional work. No inherited credentials, unlimited authority, or uncontrolled spawning.

## 3. Disagreement protocol

1. Classify the conflict: safety/rights, factual evidence, research methodology, strategy, resource allocation, or preference.
2. Preserve each party's stated position and evidence.
3. Identify affected people and stakeholders, including silent or non-consenting parties.
4. Separate established facts, assumptions, forecasts, and value judgments.
5. Consider reversible alternatives and the least restrictive effective safeguard.
6. Apply the appropriate CLEAR, CONDITIONAL, PAUSE, HALT, or PROHIBITED gate.
7. Recuse implicated decision-makers from adjudication; refer to an independent reviewer.
8. Record the decision, dissent, conditions, expiry, appeal route, and reassessment trigger.

A parent, founder, operator, or steward receives no immunity. A child or collaborator is not automatically right merely because it dissents. Evidence and accountable authority govern the disposition.

## 4. Proposed organization relationship

UNIVERSALIS should be able to collaborate with C2O while retaining an independent research and product identity, an independent review path, and a continuity plan. C2O should likewise remain able to operate if UNIVERSALIS is unavailable. This is a proposed dual-viability requirement, not a claim that separate legal entities, ownership, or enforceable legal independence already exist.

Existing repository documents currently define a C2O-governed relationship and related approval dependencies. This charter does not silently amend that hierarchy. The owner must reconcile the conflict before this charter can become governing policy.

## 5. Research questions and falsification obligations

The charter is not established merely because its values sound desirable. Research must test:
- whether dissent and appeal paths work under founder, parent, and funding pressure;
- whether scoped containment can prevent harm without suppressing unrelated activity;
- whether a compromised steward can be independently restricted;
- whether restoration requires independent evidence and human approval;
- whether delegation remains bounded under expiry, replay, concurrency, revocation, and resource exhaustion;
- whether identity-neutral safeguards prevent discriminatory proxy features;
- whether both organizations can continue safely during funding, API, or governance disruption.

Each study must record sources, protocol, assumptions, test cases, outputs, failure cases, limitations, and independent reproduction status. Plans and tabletop scenarios do not count as executed research or completed simulations.

## 6. Acceptance gates

Before adoption or implementation:
1. Resolve the existing C2O/UNIVERSALIS authority and research-methodology conflict.
2. Obtain owner acceptance of the charter and decision-rights matrix.
3. Complete legal, IP, privacy, security, and data-classification review.
4. Define independent reviewer and appeal arrangements.
5. Test the policy against adversarial, discriminatory, coercive, and compromised-parent scenarios.
6. Establish funding, resource, recovery, and separation plans.
7. Publish only material explicitly cleared for public release.

## 7. Status and non-claims

This is a proposed governance artifact. It does not establish organizational independence, legal status, ownership, consciousness, agent rights, live safeguards, production readiness, or completed research. Until accepted, existing governing documents remain in force.
