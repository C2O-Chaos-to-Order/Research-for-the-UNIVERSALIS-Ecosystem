## objective_question

When the Lipschitz constant is below 1, does the bound become uniform in duration, so that a declared tolerance can be honoured over an unbounded horizon?

## method_summary

Iterate the offset-contraction family with eps=1/4 and L=1/2, compute the geometric limit eps/(1-L), and check whether the true error stays below that limit at every step while the closed-form bound continues to grow.

## sources_or_inputs

## assumptions

X is the real plane and Y the real line, all quantities dimensionless, d_Y the absolute difference, and phi the arithmetic mean, matching the tablet's own worked example in section 5. Delta is taken as F_Y(phi(x)) minus phi(F_X(x)) in absolute value. All arithmetic is exact rationals, so no floating-point tolerance is involved. The declared domain is treated as a closed box in both coordinates.

## acceptance_or_falsification_criteria

Acceptance: the true error stays at or below eps/(1-L) at every step, and the closed-form bound is strictly increasing in n, showing that the sum form overstates a limit that is available. Falsification: any step where the error exceeds the geometric limit.

## actual_conditions

Deterministic exact-rational computation. No network, no random sampling, no external service. Cases run in separate subprocesses against a hash-locked model file.

## outcome_summary

The geometric limit was 1/2 and the true error stayed at or below it at every step, reaching 31/64. The closed form grew from 0 to 31/64 over the same steps, so for L<1 a duration-independent tolerance is available and the summed form does not express it.

## uncertainty_and_limitations

The limit bounds the true error here because the residual is constant. For a state-dependent residual the limit would bound only an asymptotic envelope, which this case does not test.
