## objective_question

Does the premise of 01.E05 survive iteration? Specifically, can a trajectory satisfy the bound while the residual bound that justifies it has already failed, so that the guarantee is vacuous before any violation is visible?

## method_summary

Iterate a non-contracting squaring family from (2,0) with eps=1 and L=4 on a declared box of [-10,10], recording at each step the true error, the claimed bound, whether the point is inside the declared domain, and whether the residual is still within eps.

## assumptions

X is the real plane and Y the real line, all quantities dimensionless, d_Y the absolute difference, and phi the arithmetic mean, matching the tablet's own worked example in section 5. Delta is taken as F_Y(phi(x)) minus phi(F_X(x)) in absolute value. All arithmetic is exact rationals, so no floating-point tolerance is involved. The declared domain is treated as a closed box in both coordinates.

## acceptance_or_falsification_criteria

Acceptance: the bound is violated at some step, and the premise is shown to fail at or before that step, establishing that the bound cannot serve as an engineering guarantee without a forward-invariance check. Falsification: the premise holds at every step up to and including the first violation, which would mean the bound fails while its justification remains intact.

## actual_conditions

Deterministic exact-rational computation. No network, no random sampling, no external service. Cases run in separate subprocesses against a hash-locked model file.

## outcome_summary

The residual exceeded eps at step 1 while the error still equalled the bound (1 = 1) and the point was still inside the declared domain. The bound was first violated at step 2 (error 7 against bound 5). The premise therefore failed one step before the bound reported any problem.

## uncertainty_and_limitations

Demonstrated on one family. The domain and L are authored inputs, and a different L on the same box would change the step numbers. Whether the premise-failure-before-bound-failure ordering generalises is untested. The expectation for this case was corrected after run 1, where my first version was arithmetically wrong; the wrong values remain on record.
