# Working Paper: 1789–1797 — Founding Republic, Revolutionary Atlantic, and Contested Authority

**Programme:** GC-R02 — Historical Decision-Making and Human Agency  
**Period:** 1789–1797  
**Status:** Preliminary working paper; evidence gathering and corroboration continue  
**Document date:** 2026-11-02

## 1. Scope and caution

This paper examines the early Washington presidency within its domestic and global setting. It includes government decision-makers, advisers, Indigenous nations, enslaved people, reformers, writers, and other affected communities.

It is not a comprehensive history of the period. It does not rank individuals or claim that every contemporaneous global event caused or influenced American policy. Causal connections require evidence.

## 2. Global and domestic context

Major developments overlapping this period included:

- The French Revolution (beginning 1789), which transformed European politics and helped produce war between France and several European powers.
- The Haitian Revolution (beginning 1791), in which enslaved people and other groups challenged slavery and French colonial rule; Haitian independence was declared in 1804, after this paper's period.
- The French Revolutionary Wars (beginning 1792), which affected American neutrality, shipping, diplomacy, and treaty debates.
- The White Lotus Rebellion in Qing China (1796–1804), a major uprising occurring during the same broad period; direct influence on Washington's decisions has not been established here.
- British, Spanish, and American imperial and territorial competition in North America.
- Indigenous nations' efforts to preserve sovereignty and territory amid United States expansion.
- Atlantic abolitionist activity, including the publication of Olaudah Equiano's autobiography in 1789 and Mary Wollstonecraft's work on women's education and rights in 1792.

The global timeline must be expanded in later passes to include African, Asian, European, Indigenous American, and other perspectives. Simultaneity alone is not causation.

## 3. Washington and the Neutrality Proclamation (1793)

### Decision

On April 22, 1793, President Washington issued a proclamation stating that the United States would pursue friendly and impartial conduct toward the European powers at war and warning U.S. citizens against conduct that would violate the law of nations.

### Decision environment

The United States had an alliance with France dating from the American Revolutionary War. France was at war with Britain and other European powers. The United States had limited military capacity and substantial commercial and diplomatic vulnerabilities.

Washington requested Cabinet advice on questions including neutrality, treaty obligations, the reception of the French minister, and whether Congress should be called. Jefferson's surviving Cabinet notes record disagreement over the continuing legal effect of the French treaties. Washington's journal records approval and issuance of the proclamation on April 22.

### Preliminary determinants

- Treaty obligations and disputed treaty interpretation.
- Risk of war with Britain or France.
- Commercial dependence and limited military capacity.
- Conflicting views within the Cabinet.
- Public sympathy for the French Revolution.
- Questions concerning executive and congressional authority.

### Outcomes and legal issues

The proclamation stated a policy of neutrality. The later Henfield prosecution and acquittal raised questions about whether the executive proclamation alone created a criminal prohibition. Congress enacted the Neutrality Act in 1794.

### Research assessment

This case illustrates the distinction between announcing foreign policy, interpreting treaty obligations, and creating enforceable criminal law. More documentary comparison is required before reaching conclusions about the relative weight of each determinant.

Sources: [Washington's Proclamation of Neutrality (Yale Avalon)](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/neutra93.asp); [Washington's presidential journal, April 22, 1793 (Founders Online search portal)](https://founders.archives.gov/); [Thomas Jefferson's Cabinet notes (Founders Online search portal)](https://founders.archives.gov/); [Henfield case historical account (Federal Judicial Center)](https://www.fjc.gov/).

## 4. Whiskey Rebellion (1791–1794)

### Decision environment

Congress enacted a federal excise on distilled spirits in 1791 as part of the federal government's response to Revolutionary War debt. Western communities faced transport difficulties, cash constraints, and a tax structure that could impose proportionally greater burdens on small-scale producers. Some residents also objected to federal priorities concerning frontier security and access to markets.

### Actors and affected parties

Relevant actors include Congress, Treasury Secretary Alexander Hamilton, President Washington, federal tax collectors, western farmers and distillers, local political organizers, peaceful petitioners, and people who used intimidation or violence.

Do not treat all western residents as insurgents or all opposition as violent. Do not treat all federal actions as automatically legitimate merely because the government asserted authority.

### Escalation and response

Resistance included petitions, public meetings, noncompliance, intimidation, assaults, and attacks on property. The administration issued warnings and attempted conciliation before mobilizing militia forces. Washington's September 25, 1794 proclamation described the administration's rationale for calling militia and its view that conciliatory measures had failed.

### Preliminary determinants

- Federal revenue needs and debt obligations.
- Unequal practical effects of the tax.
- Geography, roads, transport costs, and cash scarcity.
- Local distrust of distant government.
- Disagreement over federal jurisdiction and enforcement.
- Violent intimidation and resistance.
- The administration's concern about the credibility of federal law.

### Open questions

- How did the tax burden vary by still size, production method, and region?
- Which petitions and negotiation proposals were received, and how were they evaluated?
- Were less disruptive enforcement options available?
- How did government preparations for force affect negotiation prospects?
- What did affected communities understand about the federal law and available appeals?

Sources: [Washington's September 25, 1794 proclamation (Miller Center)](https://millercenter.org/the-presidency/presidential-speeches/september-25-1794-proclamation-militia-service); [Washington's September 25, 1794 proclamation (Yale Avalon)](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/gwproc10.asp); [U.S. Treasury historical account of the Whiskey Rebellion](https://home.treasury.gov/).

## 5. Indigenous sovereignty and the Treaty of Greenville (1795)

### Context

Indigenous nations in the Northwest Territory were political communities with their own governments, diplomatic relationships, territorial claims, and internal differences. United States settlement and military expansion generated conflict.

The Northwest Ordinance of 1787 stated that Indigenous lands and property should not be taken without consent. The subsequent Northwest Indian War and the Treaty of Greenville require examination against that stated protection.

### Treaty

The Treaty of Greenville, concluded August 3, 1795, involved the United States and twelve named Indigenous nations or political communities: Wyandot, Delaware, Shawnee, Ottawa, Chippewa, Potawatomi, Miami, Eel River, Wea, Kickapoo, Piankashaw, and Kaskaskia.

The treaty ended hostilities, established boundaries, and included extensive territorial cessions to the United States. It also contained provisions concerning prisoners, trade, and the treatment of Indigenous communities.

### Analytical requirements

- Identify each participating nation and its representatives.
- Examine translation, authority to negotiate, and the circumstances of treaty consent.
- Distinguish the political positions of different Indigenous leaders.
- Examine military pressure and bargaining asymmetry.
- Trace implementation and subsequent settlement.
- Avoid treating an agreement by named representatives as proof of unanimous consent among all affected people.

### Correction

Do not describe Little Turtle as the principal commander at the Battle of Fallen Timbers. Blue Jacket is generally identified as the principal Indigenous commander. Little Turtle's views on negotiation and his role in the Treaty of Greenville require careful source comparison.

Sources: [Northwest Ordinance (National Archives)](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/northwest-ordinance); [Treaty of Greenville, original treaty text (DocsTeach/National Archives)](https://docsteach.org/document/treaty-greenville/); [Treaty of Greenville (Yale Avalon)](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/greenvil.asp).

## 6. Enslavement and the Washington household

### Context and scale

The 1790 federal census recorded 697,681 enslaved people out of a total population of 3,929,214, approximately 17.8 percent. This corrects an earlier working figure of 694,280 and 17.67 percent. Census categories and enumerated populations must be described using the original historical definitions.

### Washington's conduct

Washington's private views, public actions, household practices, and eventual will must be examined separately. Mount Vernon documents his use of rotation practices to avoid Pennsylvania's gradual abolition law, as well as his later provisions for freeing people he personally owned through his will. These facts should not be collapsed into a simple claim that he either opposed or supported slavery.

### Ona Judge

Ona Judge escaped from the Washington household on May 21, 1796. Mount Vernon's account documents her later recollection, the May 24, 1796 newspaper advertisement seeking her return, and Washington's subsequent effort to have her returned through officials in New Hampshire.

Her 1845 interview is retrospective testimony recorded decades after the escape. Washington's 1796 correspondence is contemporary evidence of his response. Both sources are valuable but answer different questions.

### Hercules Posey

Mount Vernon's research identifies Hercules's escape on February 22, 1797. Washington subsequently sought information that might lead to his return. Hercules's daughter Eve reportedly expressed happiness that her father was free. This family perspective must remain visible alongside Washington's correspondence.

### Analytical requirements

- Examine the authority claimed by enslavers and the absence of meaningful autonomy for enslaved people.
- Preserve the agency, plans, relationships, and decisions of Ona Judge, Hercules, and their families.
- Distinguish legal status from moral legitimacy.
- Do not treat a person's expressed reservations about slavery as equivalent to ending their participation in enslavement.
- Investigate the role of state law, federal law, household practices, and escape networks.

Sources: [Ona Judge (Mount Vernon)](https://www.mountvernon.org/library/digitalhistory/digital-encyclopedia/article/ona-judge); [Hercules Posey (Mount Vernon)](https://www.mountvernon.org/library/digitalhistory/digital-encyclopedia/article/hercules); [1790 slave population table (U.S. Census Bureau historical compendium)](https://www2.census.gov/prod2/decennial/documents/1870e-02.pdf).

## 7. Knowledge, testimony, and reform

### Olaudah Equiano

Equiano's autobiography appeared in 1789 and became part of British and Atlantic abolitionist advocacy. It is a significant example of firsthand testimony and public writing being used to challenge institutions.

Historians have debated details of his early-life narrative by comparing his autobiography with other records. That debate should be recorded without dismissing the documented historical significance of his abolitionist activity.

### Mary Wollstonecraft

Wollstonecraft's 1792 *A Vindication of the Rights of Woman* challenged social and educational arrangements that restricted women's intellectual development. It is evidence of contemporary criticism of gender hierarchy and the argument that education should develop reason and independent judgment.

Sources: [Equiano's autobiography (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15399); [Wollstonecraft's 1792 work (Library of Congress)](https://www.loc.gov/item/34033009/); [1792 text (University of Virginia)](https://anthologydev.lib.virginia.edu/work/Wollstonecraft/wollstonecraft-vindication-excerpted).

## 8. Preliminary findings

This period provides evidence for several recurring questions, but not yet universal causal laws:

- Policy design can create unequal practical burdens even when a rule is formally general.
- Executive announcements, legislation, and judicial enforcement are distinct forms of authority.
- Leaders depend on advisers whose assumptions and interests may conflict.
- Treaty-making must be analyzed from the perspectives of all parties, including communities with less military or diplomatic leverage.
- People denied formal power can still exercise agency, develop strategies, preserve knowledge, and challenge institutions.
- Expressed principles should be compared with actual conduct and material consequences.

## 9. Corrections and unresolved matters

- Ona Judge's escape date is May 21, 1796.
- Hercules Posey's escape is dated February 22, 1797, by Mount Vernon's research.
- Little Turtle should not be identified as the principal commander at Fallen Timbers.
- The 1790 enslaved population count used here is 697,681, approximately 17.8 percent of the total population.
- The influence of the Haitian Revolution on American policy must be established case by case; temporal overlap is not sufficient.
- Further documentary examination is required for Cabinet deliberations, Whiskey Rebellion negotiations, treaty implementation, and the experiences of affected Indigenous communities.

## 10. Status

This working paper is a preliminary synthesis. It is not an exhaustive source audit, independent peer review, completed validation, or simulation. Claims and interpretations remain open to correction as additional primary records and specialist scholarship are examined.
