# Golden Compass Evidence and Source Register

**Programme:** GC-R02 — Historical Decision-Making and Human Agency  
**Register version:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** Initial linked register; not exhaustive  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-02

## Purpose and use

This register identifies source collections and documents used or proposed for the preliminary working papers covering 1789–1797 and 1797–1825.

A link in this register does not mean that every assertion on the linked page has been independently verified. Sources are identified by type and intended use. Each claim in a future formal report should be linked to the particular document, page, article, archival identifier, or passage that supports it.

The register distinguishes primary documents and legal texts; contemporary testimony and later recollections; archival and institutional descriptions; secondary historical scholarship; and research portals. Primary sources are not inherently neutral. Assess authorship, date, audience, purpose, provenance, completeness, translation, bias, and relationship to the event.

## D. Evidence and documentation gaps

The register still needs exact document-level sources for:

- Whiskey Rebellion petitions, negotiation correspondence, and local testimony.
- The full documentary record of Adams's policy toward Toussaint Louverture.
- Jefferson administration correspondence on Haiti and the 1806 restrictions.
- Treaty of Fort Jackson original records and Creek perspectives.
- Tecumseh's correspondence and speeches, with careful attention to transcription and later retellings.
- First Seminole War orders, proceedings, and cabinet correspondence.
- The experiences of free and enslaved African Americans during the War of 1812.
- Chinese, South Asian, African, and Latin American primary materials for the global timeline.

The general portals above are discovery aids, not substitutes for those specific sources.

## E. Research rules

1. Each formal report must cite claim-level sources, not merely this index.
2. Record exact title, author or issuing body, date, archival collection, identifier, stable URL, access date, and relevant passage or page.
3. Preserve original-language documents where possible and identify translators.
4. Record differences between editions and transcriptions.
5. A primary source is evidence of what its author or institution said or did; it is not automatically proof that every claim in it is true.
6. Add Indigenous-authored and community-held archival material wherever possible.
7. Mark missing, retrospective, incomplete, translated, or contested sources explicitly.
8. Maintain a correction history rather than silently replacing prior conclusions.
