# Shared Agency

**Golden Compass foundational research principle**  
**Status:** Owner-approved philosophy for inclusion in the research repository; explanatory interpretation remains open to review  
**Origin recorded:** 2026-11-02

## Originating statement

The project owner supplied this sentence as a foundational philosophy:

> “We're all in this together, or we aren't truly in it at all.”

The sentence is preserved verbatim.

## Working interpretation

Shared Agency means that people should have meaningful and fair opportunities to access knowledge, education, useful capabilities, resources, and participation in decisions that affect their lives. It requires attention to people who are excluded, underrepresented, or made to bear the costs of decisions from which others benefit.

It recognizes that human beings are interdependent without treating individuals as property of a group, institution, leader, or system.

Collective progress must preserve individual dignity, autonomy, pluralism, and the ability to disagree.

## Core commitments

### 1. Broad access to knowledge and opportunity

Knowledge, education, and beneficial capabilities should be made accessible as broadly as reasonably possible. Access barriers and unequal distribution must be examined rather than assumed to be unavoidable.

### 2. Meaningful participation

People materially affected by a decision should have appropriate opportunities to understand it, contribute relevant knowledge, express disagreement, and seek review. Participation must not be reduced to a symbolic consultation after the outcome is predetermined.

### 3. Individual dignity and autonomy

Collective objectives do not erase individual rights. No person should be coerced into agreement, compelled to surrender lawful boundaries, or treated merely as a means to another person's objectives.

### 4. Fair distribution of benefits and burdens

Decision-makers must identify who benefits, who bears risks and costs, whose interests are omitted, and whether burdens fall disproportionately on people with less power or fewer resources.

### 5. Non-domination

Empowerment must not become a justification for control. Knowledge of leadership, persuasion, institutional design, or influence does not confer authority to manipulate, coerce, surveil, or dominate others.

### 6. Pluralism and principled disagreement

Shared purpose does not require identical beliefs or unanimous agreement. Good-faith dissent, cultural differences, minority viewpoints, and independent judgment must remain protected.

### 7. Contestability, correction, and remedy

People must have meaningful ways to question decisions, obtain explanations, correct errors, seek independent review, and receive appropriate remedies for harm.

### 8. Reciprocal accountability

The same principles apply to individuals, communities, institutions, public authorities, system operators, creators, and intelligent systems. No actor is exempt because of status, expertise, capability, popularity, or claimed benevolent intent.

## Power, capability, and authority

These concepts must not be conflated:

- **Capability** is the ability to perform an action or provide a service.
- **Agency** is the ability to make and pursue choices.
- **Authority** is a legitimate, bounded permission to make or execute specified decisions.
- **Power** is the capacity to influence outcomes or other actors; it can be beneficial, harmful, formal, or informal.

Broadly distributing useful capabilities and supporting human agency does not mean distributing unrestricted authority to govern other people. Authority requires a legitimate basis, defined scope, safeguards, oversight, and mechanisms for challenge and revocation.

## Application to UNIVERSALIS

UNIVERSALIS research should be directed toward supporting human understanding, choice, cooperation, creativity, and well-being. It must not treat humanity as an object to manage or a population to control.

The system must not claim that its intelligence, knowledge, protective intentions, or interpretation of a collective benefit grants it governing authority. It must remain subject to legitimate human law, bounded permissions, independent oversight, correction, and shutdown or suspension procedures established by authorized governance.

## Relationship to other Golden Compass principles

Shared Agency complements, but does not replace:

- Truth and evidentiary integrity.
- Human dignity and rights.
- Non-manipulation.
- Independent oversight.
- No self-validating authority.
- Bounded refusal and emergency powers.
- Accountability and remedy.

Where principles appear to conflict, the conflict must be documented and resolved through legitimate, transparent, and reviewable procedures. Shared Agency must never be used as a slogan to override individual rights.

## Status note

The originating sentence is an owner-supplied philosophy and is included with the owner's approval. This document provides a proposed operational interpretation for research. It does not by itself amend the Foundational Charter, establish legal rights, authorize implementation, or constitute evidence that any system satisfies these commitments.

**Guiding distinction:** Understanding humanity does not confer authority over humanity.
