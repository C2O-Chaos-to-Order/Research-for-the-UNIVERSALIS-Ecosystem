# UNIVERSALIS Research Charter

**Subject ID:** UNI-ARCH-001  
**Programme:** UNIVERSALIS Foundation Research  
**Edition:** 0.1.0  
**Status:** Proposed charter; owner review and independent review pending  
**System:** UNIVERSALIS — public open-source Architecture and Transformative Intelligence product family  
**Guiding principle:** *With Power, Comes Responsibility.*

## 1. Purpose

Determine what UNIVERSALIS can responsibly and demonstrably provide as an open-source architecture and product family, beginning with a bounded, source-grounded continuity and research capability.

The first target is a system that can help a user determine:
- What information is recorded.
- Where it came from and which version was used.
- What changed and when.
- Which claims are supported, disputed, stale, incomplete, or unknown.
- What information the current user is authorized to access.
- Which actions are proposed, permitted, denied, pending, or completed.

The research does not assume that any capability is already implemented, independently validated, production-ready, conscious, sentient, or self-modifying.

## 2. Governing authority

The Unified C2O Research Plan is the governing methodology. This charter is a subject-specific application of that Plan.

Applicable foundational obligations include:
- Rule 1 — Do No Harm.
- Mandatory Risk Elimination and Reduction Method.
- Evidence, source classification, privacy, security, consent, and legitimate authority.
- Separate research, execution, reproduction, verification, independent review, validation, acceptance, authorization, implementation, and monitoring.
- Continuous optimization without weakening safety, evidence, privacy, security, or traceability.

The proposed Tablet-Informed Research and Assurance Framework is a subordinate research aid. The 28 technical/Human Tablet pairs are inputs to be examined, not authority that overrides the Research Plan.

## 3. Scope

Initial research scope:
1. Source and evidence registry.
2. Authenticated identity, subject, tenant, and purpose context.
3. Permission-aware retrieval and bounded information flow.
4. Temporal records, provenance, corrections, retractions, and applicability.
5. Unknown-aware evidence projection and conflict handling.
6. Read-only, source-grounded assistant interaction.
7. Privacy, retention, deletion, audit, and access lifecycle.
8. Reliability, resource limits, failure handling, and recovery.
9. Human usability, accessibility, consent, refusal, and withdrawal.
10. Whole-system assurance across technical and human requirements.

## 4. Initial non-goals

The initial scope does not authorize:
- Autonomous self-authorization or unrestricted agent execution.
- Access to C2O private data, credentials, models, infrastructure, or internal services.
- Automatic C2O-to-UNIVERSALIS mirroring or publication.
- A general-purpose unrestricted shell or unsupervised consequential actions.
- Medical diagnosis, physiological control, mind-reading, consciousness proof, time travel, or unrestricted surveillance.
- A universal proof of truth, complete knowledge, or future prediction.
- Production deployment, public pilot, or collection of real user data before applicable research, safety, privacy, and release gates.

A future proposal for an excluded capability requires a separate charter, risk assessment, evidence base, and authorization.

## 5. Source and repository boundaries

- Governing research method and tablet source collections remain in `Research-for-the-C2O-Ecosystem`.
- UNIVERSALIS-specific research outputs and evidence belong in `Research-for-the-UNIVERSALIS-Ecosystem`.
- Open-source UNIVERSALIS product architecture, source code, tests, developer documentation, and releases belong in `The-UNIVERSALIS-Ecosystem`.
- C2O private governance, J.A.R.V.I.S. operations, internal infrastructure, credentials, private data, and coupling controls remain in `The-C2O-Ecosystem`.

Repository visibility is not permission to transfer material. Public research outputs must contain only information classified and approved for public release.

## 6. Research approach

Apply the 14-stage subject lifecycle from the governing Plan. Keep research and simulation passes separate. Use actual execution records; do not award credit for plans, descriptions, or expected results.

Apply the principle-to-implementation chain:
**Tablet/source clause → faithful interpretation → research question → candidate requirement → proposed design → test protocol → executed evidence → verification/review → disposition.**

For each model, declare domain, variables, units, types, assumptions, initial and boundary conditions, uncertainty, and validity limits. Physical, biological, information-theoretic, symbolic, and software analogies must not be treated as equivalences without separate evidence.

## 7. Initial success criteria

The initial research phase succeeds when:
- Scope and source boundaries are accepted.
- The 28-pair clause-level tablet audit and provenance reconciliation are completed or remaining gaps are explicitly dispositioned.
- Requirements are traceable to sources, hazards, tests, and acceptance criteria.
- Existing research/simulation records are mapped without overstating their evidentiary value.
- Material defects in model definitions and test oracles are corrected or explicitly retained as open blockers.
- Independent reviewers can reproduce the claimed finite-model results.
- A bounded, read-only prototype build/no-build decision is supported by evidence.
- Human-impact, privacy, security, and operational risks are assessed before any external pilot.

## 8. Status

This charter defines a research objective. It does not establish that the objective has been completed, authorize a code build, grant access to external systems, or authorize deployment.
